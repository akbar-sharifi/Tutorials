## Core Philosophy

### The Central Insight

**Traditional management**: Give advice, solve problems, tell people what to do
**Coaching approach**: Ask questions, build capability, help people solve their own problems

**Why this matters**:
- Creates independent, capable team members
- Reduces your workload long-term
- Builds engagement and ownership
- Develops problem-solving skills in others

### The Two Mantras

**1. "Stay curious a little longer"**
- Resist the urge to jump to solutions
- Ask one more question before giving advice
- Explore the issue more deeply

**2. "Rush to action and advice a little slower"**
- Your first solution is rarely the best solution
- The real problem often isn't the first problem mentioned
- Understanding comes before solving

## The Advice Monster

### What Is It?

The **Advice Monster** is your inner voice that says: *"I know the answer! Let me tell you what to do!"*

It appears in three forms:

**1. Tell-It**
- "I need to have all the answers"
- Compulsion to solve every problem
- Proving your expertise/value

**2. Save-It**
- "I need to rescue everyone"
- Taking on others' problems as your own
- Protecting people from failure

**3. Control-It**
- "I need things done my way"
- Micromanaging
- Difficulty delegating truly

### Why It's Problematic

**For you**:
- Creates dependency (people keep coming back)
- Overwork and burnout
- Bottleneck in the team

**For them**:
- Stunts growth and development
- Reduces ownership and engagement
- Prevents learning

### Taming the Advice Monster

**Step 1: Notice it**
- Recognize when you're about to give advice
- Feel the urge to jump in

**Step 2: Pause**
- Take a breath
- Count to three

**Step 3: Ask a question instead**
- Any of the seven questions
- Start with "And what else?"

**You don't eliminate it—you manage it.**

## The Seven Essential Questions

### 1. The Kickstart Question

**"What's on your mind?"**

**Purpose**: Open any conversation efficiently

**Why it works**:
- Gets to the real issue quickly
- Avoids small talk that wastes time
- Gives them control of the conversation
- Works in any context

**When to use**:
- Starting any coaching conversation
- Check-ins with team members
- When someone seems distracted or troubled

**Follow-up pattern**:
```
"What's on your mind?"
    ↓
They share something
    ↓
"And what else?" (see Question 2)
```

**Variations**:
- "What's on your mind today?"
- "What's the real challenge here for you?"
- "What would be most useful to talk about?"

---

### 2. The AWE Question

**"And what else?"**

**Purpose**: The single most powerful coaching question

**Why it works**:
- First answer is rarely the only answer (or best answer)
- Slows down the rush to action
- Uncovers deeper issues
- Buys you time (you don't need to have the answer)

**When to use**:
- After almost any answer
- When you feel pressure to give advice
- To explore an issue more fully
- 3-5 times per conversation (then it gets annoying)

**The magic**:
```
Q: "What's on your mind?"
A: "The project deadline is too tight."

Q: "And what else?"
A: "The team doesn't have the right skills."

Q: "And what else?"
A: "Actually, I'm not sure we're building the right thing."

← The real issue emerges
```

**Stop asking when**:
- They say "There's nothing else"
- You've asked it 5 times
- The energy drops (they're scraping the barrel)

**Pro tip**: This question works everywhere—meetings, one-on-ones, home conversations.

---

### 3. The Focus Question

**"What's the real challenge here for you?"**

**Purpose**: Cut to the core issue that matters to this person

**Why it works**:
- **"Real"**: Acknowledges there are multiple challenges
- **"Challenge"**: Frames it as solvable, not a disaster
- **"Here"**: Makes it specific, not abstract
- **"For you"**: Makes it personal and actionable

**When to use**:
- When drowning in complexity
- When they're talking about others' problems
- To redirect to what they can control
- After exploring with "And what else?"

**Common scenario**:
```
Person: "The client is demanding, the team is behind,
        and management keeps changing requirements."

You: "What's the real challenge here for you?"

Person: "I guess... I don't know how to push back on
        the client without damaging the relationship."

← Now you have something workable
```

**Key insight**: People often talk about "out there" problems they can't control. This question brings focus to what *they* can do.

---

### 4. The Foundation Question

**"What do you want?"**

**Purpose**: Clarify desired outcomes and uncover hidden agendas

**Why it works**:
- Forces clarity on goals
- Reveals what's really motivating them
- Shifts from complaining to solution-finding

**When to use**:
- When the conversation is circular
- When they're venting without direction
- To surface competing desires
- Before offering help

**Variations**:
- "What do you want from me?"
- "What does success look like?"
- "What outcome are you hoping for?"

**The two-part structure**:

**Part 1**: "What do you want?" (their stated desire)
**Part 2**: "And what else do you want?" (the hidden desire)

**Example**:
```
Q: "What do you want?"
A: "I want the project delivered on time."

Q: "And what else do you want?"
A: "I want to prove I can lead complex projects."

← The real motivation surfaces
```

**Caution**: This can feel confrontational. Use warm tone, genuine curiosity.

---

### 5. The Lazy Question

**"How can I help?"**

**Purpose**: Prevent doing work that isn't yours

**Why it's "lazy"**:
- Stops you from guessing what help they need
- Prevents over-commitment
- Makes them articulate the request

**When to use**:
- Before offering to help
- When someone brings you a problem
- When you feel yourself taking on their work

**The problem it solves**:
```
Without it:
Person: "We're struggling with the database."
You: "I'll take a look tonight."
← You just took on their problem

With it:
Person: "We're struggling with the database."
You: "How can I help?"
Person: "Could you review our schema design?"
← Specific, bounded request
```

**Variations**:
- "What do you need from me?"
- "What support would be useful?"
- "What would be most helpful?"

**Follow-up** (critical):
After they answer, ask: **"Is there anything else I can do for you?"**

Often the answer is **"No"** — and you've just avoided over-committing.

---

### 6. The Strategic Question

**"If you're saying yes to this, what are you saying no to?"**

**Purpose**: Make the trade-offs visible

**Why it works**:
- Everything has an opportunity cost
- Forces prioritization
- Prevents over-commitment
- Makes implicit trade-offs explicit

**When to use**:
- When adding new commitments
- In prioritization conversations
- When someone is overwhelmed
- Before saying yes to requests

**The insight**:
```
Saying yes to:          Means saying no to:
- New project          → Current project deadlines
- This meeting         → Focused work time
- This feature         → That feature
- Helping this person  → Other responsibilities
```

**Application to yourself**:
Before taking on something new, ask yourself this question.

**Variations**:
- "What will you stop doing to make room for this?"
- "What's the cost of saying yes to this?"
- "What gets de-prioritized?"

**Deeper version**:
**"If you're saying yes to this, what are you saying no to?"**
Then: **"What's important about that?"**
And: **"What's important about *that*?"**

Keep asking "What's important about that?" to reach core values.

---

### 7. The Learning Question

**"What was most useful for you?"**

**Purpose**: End every conversation by extracting the learning

**Why it works**:
- Makes learning explicit
- Tells you what actually helped
- Reinforces key insights
- Builds self-awareness

**When to use**:
- End of every coaching conversation (make it a ritual)
- After meetings
- After projects
- In retrospectives

**The discipline**:
```
Every conversation should end:
"Before we finish, what was most useful for you?"
```

**What it does**:
1. **For them**: Identifies the insight they'll remember and apply
2. **For you**: Shows what's actually landing (often surprising)

**Common answers**:
- "Realizing I was trying to solve the wrong problem"
- "The permission to say no"
- "Just talking it through helped me see it clearly"

**Follow-up**:
Don't add anything after they answer. Let the insight land.

**Resist the urge to**:
- Add more advice ("And you should also...")
- Diminish it ("Oh, that was just...")
- Make it about you ("I'm glad I could help...")

Just say: **"Great. Thanks."** And end.

## The Question Framework

### The Basic Coaching Conversation Structure

```
1. "What's on your mind?"
   (Get them talking)
   ↓
2. "And what else?" × 3-5
   (Explore fully)
   ↓
3. "What's the real challenge here for you?"
   (Find focus)
   ↓
4. "What do you want?"
   (Clarify outcome)
   ↓
5. "How can I help?"
   (Define your role)
   ↓
6. "If you're saying yes to this, what are you saying no to?"
   (Acknowledge trade-offs)
   ↓
7. "What was most useful for you?"
   (Extract learning)
```

**Time required**: 10 minutes (informal coaching)

### The 3P Framework (Going Deeper)

When exploring "What's on your mind?", guide them through three levels:

**Project**: The practical problem or situation
- "What's the project we're talking about?"

**People**: Relationships involved
- "Who else is involved in this?"
- "What's going on between you and [person]?"

**Pattern**: Recurring behavior or situation
- "What patterns do you notice?"
- "Have you seen this before?"

**The deepest work happens at the Pattern level.**

Example:
```
Project: "The client keeps changing requirements."
People: "I'm frustrated with my manager for not protecting us."
Pattern: "I struggle to set boundaries and say no."
← This is where growth happens
```

## Building the Habit

### The 3-Step Habit Formula

To build any new habit (like asking questions):

**1. Trigger**: "When [this happens]..."
**2. New Behavior**: "I will [do this]..."
**3. Reward**: "So I can [get this benefit]..."

**Example**:
- **Trigger**: When someone brings me a problem
- **New Behavior**: I will ask "What's on your mind?" and "And what else?"
- **Reward**: So I can avoid taking on work that isn't mine

### Start Small

**Don't try all seven questions at once.**

**Week 1-2**: Master "And what else?"
- Use it 5 times per day
- In any conversation
- Notice the results

**Week 3-4**: Add "What's on your mind?"
- Start conversations with this
- Follow with "And what else?"

**Week 5-6**: Add "What was most useful for you?"
- End coaching conversations with this
- Every single time

**Month 2-3**: Layer in the others as appropriate

### Practice Scenarios

**Low-stakes practice**:
- Friends and family
- Casual conversations
- When you have nothing at risk

**Why**: Build muscle memory before high-pressure situations.

**With yourself**:
Ask yourself the questions:
- "What's on my mind?"
- "And what else?"
- "What's the real challenge here for me?"

Self-coaching is powerful.

## Common Pitfalls and Solutions

### Pitfall 1: Asking Questions But Still Giving Advice

**What it looks like**:
"What's on your mind? ... And what else? ... Okay, here's what you should do..."

**The problem**: You're just delaying the Advice Monster, not taming it.

**Solution**: Ask one more question instead of giving advice:
- "What have you already tried?"
- "What options are you considering?"
- "What would you do if I weren't here?"

### Pitfall 2: Making Statements Disguised as Questions

**What it looks like**:
- "Have you thought about trying X?" (You're suggesting X)
- "Don't you think you should...?" (You're telling them what to do)

**The problem**: These aren't questions; they're advice with a question mark.

**Solution**: Ask genuine open questions:
- "What options are you exploring?"
- "What might you try?"

### Pitfall 3: Asking "Why?"

**What it looks like**:
"Why did you do that?"

**The problem**: "Why" often sounds judgmental and puts people on the defensive.

**Solution**: Use "What" instead:
- "What led to that decision?"
- "What were you hoping would happen?"
- "What's important about that?"

### Pitfall 4: Asking Too Many Questions

**What it looks like**:
Rapid-fire interrogation without listening to answers.

**The problem**: Feels like cross-examination, not coaching.

**Solution**:
- Ask one question
- **Actually listen** to the full answer
- Pause before the next question
- Maximum: One question per minute

### Pitfall 5: Having the Answer Already in Mind

**What it looks like**:
Asking questions while already knowing what you want them to say.

**The problem**: You're manipulating, not coaching.

**Solution**: Genuine curiosity. If you can't be curious, be honest:
"I have a suggestion. Do you want to hear it?"

### Pitfall 6: Skipping the Learning Question

**What it looks like**:
Conversation ends without "What was most useful for you?"

**The problem**: The learning doesn't stick; they don't internalize insights.

**Solution**: Make it a non-negotiable ritual. Every. Single. Time.

## Practical Application Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Someone Brings You a Problem

**Traditional approach**:
```
Employee: "The customer is upset about the delay."
Manager: "Okay, I'll call them and sort it out."
```

**Coaching approach**:
```
Employee: "The customer is upset about the delay."
Manager: "What's on your mind about this?"
Employee: "I'm worried they'll cancel the contract."
Manager: "And what else?"
Employee: "I'm not sure what to tell them."
Manager: "What's the real challenge here for you?"
Employee: "I guess... I don't know how to have difficult
         conversations with upset customers."
Manager: "What do you want to get better at?"
Employee: "Handling these situations confidently."
Manager: "How can I help?"
Employee: "Could you role-play a difficult conversation with me?"
Manager: "Yes. Before we finish, what was most useful?"
Employee: "Realizing it's not about the delay—it's about
         my confidence in these conversations."
```

**Result**: They own the problem and grow capability.

### Scenario 2: Overwhelmed Team Member

**Traditional approach**:
```
Employee: "I'm drowning. Too much on my plate."
Manager: "Let me take the Smith project off your hands."
```

**Coaching approach**:
```
Employee: "I'm drowning. Too much on my plate."
Manager: "What's on your mind about this?"
Employee: "I have five projects and they're all urgent."
Manager: "And what else?"
Employee: "I haven't said no to anything in months."
Manager: "What's the real challenge here for you?"
Employee: "I'm afraid I'll disappoint people if I say no."
Manager: "What do you want?"
Employee: "I want to focus on high-impact work."
Manager: "If you're saying yes to focusing on high-impact
         work, what are you saying no to?"
Employee: "I guess... I'm saying no to some of these five
         projects."
Manager: "How can I help you figure out which ones?"
```

**Result**: They learn prioritization, not just this-time relief.

### Scenario 3: Request for Your Time

**Traditional approach**:
```
Colleague: "Can you join this project team?"
You: "Sure, when do we meet?"
```

**Coaching approach**:
```
Colleague: "Can you join this project team?"
You: "What do you need from me specifically?"
Colleague: "We need someone who understands the tech architecture."
You: "How can I help with that?"
Colleague: "Maybe you could review our design document?"
You: "That I can do. Is there anything else?"
Colleague: "No, that would be perfect."
You: "Great. If I'm saying yes to this review, I'm
     saying no to finishing my report until next week.
     That work for you?"
```

**Result**: Bounded commitment, clear expectations.

### Scenario 4: Performance Issue

**Traditional approach**:
```
Manager: "Your reports have been late three times.
         You need to manage your time better."
```

**Coaching approach**:
```
Manager: "I've noticed your reports have been late
         three times this month. What's on your mind?"
Employee: "I've been really busy."
Manager: "And what else?"
Employee: "The data collection takes longer than expected."
Manager: "What's the real challenge here for you?"
Employee: "I don't want to ask for help because I should
         be able to handle this."
Manager: "What do you want?"
Employee: "I want to deliver on time and maintain quality."
Manager: "How can I help?"
Employee: "Maybe we could discuss the data collection process?"
```

**Result**: Gets to root cause, preserves dignity.

### Scenario 5: Self-Coaching

**When stuck on a decision**:
```
You: "What's on my mind?"
You: "Should I take this new job offer."
You: "And what else?"
You: "I'm worried about leaving my team."
You: "What's the real challenge here for me?"
You: "I'm torn between growth and loyalty."
You: "What do I want?"
You: "I want growth without feeling like I'm abandoning people."
You: "If I say yes to this job, what am I saying no to?"
You: "Daily connection with people I care about."
You: "What's important about that?"
You: "Those relationships matter to me."
You: "What's the learning here?"
You: "This isn't really about the job—it's about
     maintaining relationships through change."
```

## Quick Reference Guide

### The Seven Questions at a Glance

| Question | Purpose | When to Use |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| **"What's on your mind?"** | Start conversations | Beginning of any conversation |
| **"And what else?"** | Explore deeper | After any answer, 3-5 times |
| **"What's the real challenge here for you?"** | Find focus | When diving deeper, to get specific |
| **"What do you want?"** | Clarify outcome | When conversation is circular |
| **"How can I help?"** | Define your role | Before offering help |
| **"If you're saying yes to this, what are you saying no to?"** | Reveal trade-offs | During prioritization, commitments |
| **"What was most useful for you?"** | Extract learning | End of every conversation |

### Daily Practice Checklist

**Morning**:
- [ ] Set intention: "Today I'll ask 'And what else?' 5 times"

**During the day**:
- [ ] Notice when Advice Monster appears
- [ ] Pause before giving advice
- [ ] Ask a question instead

**Evening**:
- [ ] Reflect: When did I coach well?
- [ ] What could I have asked instead of advising?
- [ ] Self-coaching: "What was most useful for me today?"

### The 10-Minute Coaching Conversation

```
0:00 - "What's on your mind?"
      Listen fully to answer

0:02 - "And what else?" × 3
      Each time, listen fully

0:05 - "What's the real challenge here for you?"
      Listen, resist solving

0:07 - "What do you want?" or "How can I help?"
      Get specific

0:09 - "What was most useful for you?"
      Listen, don't add anything

0:10 - End. Thank them.
```

### Troubleshooting Guide

| If this happens... | Try this... |
|-------------------|-------------|
| They say "I don't know" | "If you did know, what would you say?" |
| They keep talking about others | "What's the challenge here for *you*?" |
| You feel stuck | "And what else?" |
| They want you to solve it | "What have you already tried?" |
| You want to give advice | Ask one more question |
| Running out of time | "What was most useful?" (never skip) |
| They're venting | "What do you want?" |

## Key Principles to Remember

### 1. Questions Over Advice

**Bad**: "Here's what you should do..."
**Good**: "What options are you considering?"

### 2. Curiosity Over Knowing

**Bad**: Asking questions while already having the answer
**Good**: Genuine interest in their thinking

### 3. Their Problem, Their Solution

**Bad**: Taking on their work
**Good**: Helping them find their own solution

### 4. Short Conversations, Regular Practice

**Bad**: Waiting for formal "coaching sessions"
**Good**: 10 minutes daily, in regular conversations

### 5. Listen More, Talk Less

**Bad**: Doing 70% of the talking
**Good**: They do 70% of the talking

### 6. Build Independence, Not Dependence

**Bad**: Becoming the go-to problem solver
**Good**: Building their problem-solving capability

### 7. Make Learning Explicit

**Bad**: Ending without reflection
**Good**: "What was most useful for you?" every time

## Summary: Your Coaching Transformation

### Before Coaching Habit

- Overwhelmed with others' problems
- Team dependent on you for answers
- Spending time on others' work
- Feeling like you must have all answers
- Reactive, always firefighting

### After Coaching Habit

- Others solve their own problems
- Team is independent and capable
- Focus on your actual work
- Comfortable with "I don't know"
- Proactive, developing people

### The Core Practice

1. **Notice** the Advice Monster
2. **Pause** before responding
3. **Ask** one of the seven questions
4. **Listen** fully to the answer
5. **Repeat** until they have clarity
6. **End** with "What was most useful?"

### Start Today

**Your first step**:
Pick ONE question. Just one.

Most people start with: **"And what else?"**

Use it 5 times today. That's it.

Tomorrow, use it again.

In two weeks, you'll notice a difference.

In two months, it will be automatic.

In six months, your whole leadership style will have shifted.
