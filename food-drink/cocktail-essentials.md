## The Essential Bar Setup

### Minimal Equipment (Start Here)

**The Must-Haves** (can make 90% of cocktails):

1. **Cocktail shaker** - Boston shaker (tin + glass) or cobbler shaker (all-in-one)
2. **Jigger** - For measuring (1 oz and 2 oz sides)
3. **Bar spoon** - Long handle for stirring
4. **Strainer** - Hawthorne strainer (spring edge)
5. **Citrus juicer** - Hand juicer or reamer
6. **Muddler** - For crushing herbs/fruit
7. **Paring knife** - For garnishes
8. **Cutting board**

**Cost**: ~$50-75 for the whole set

**Glassware Essentials**:
- Rocks glass (old fashioned)
- Coupe or martini glass
- Highball glass
- That's it to start

### Nice-to-Haves (Add Later)

- Fine mesh strainer (for double straining)
- Peeler (for twists)
- Ice molds (for large cubes)
- Mixing glass (for stirred drinks)

## Core Techniques

### 1. Shaking

**When to shake**: Drinks with juice, cream, egg whites, or thick ingredients

**How**:
1. Add ingredients to shaker
2. Fill with ice (¾ full)
3. Shake hard for 10-15 seconds
4. Strain into glass

**Why**: Chills, dilutes, and aerates (makes it frothy)

**Examples**: Margarita, Daiquiri, Whiskey Sour

### 2. Stirring

**When to stir**: Spirit-forward drinks (all alcohol, no juice)

**How**:
1. Add ingredients to mixing glass (or shaker tin)
2. Fill with ice
3. Stir gently for 20-30 seconds
4. Strain into glass

**Why**: Chills and dilutes without aeration (keeps it silky)

**Examples**: Martini, Manhattan, Negroni

### 3. Building

**When to build**: Simple drinks served over ice

**How**:
1. Add ice to serving glass
2. Pour ingredients directly into glass
3. Stir briefly

**Why**: Easy, no extra equipment needed

**Examples**: Gin & Tonic, Old Fashioned (sometimes), Rum & Coke

### 4. Muddling

**When to muddle**: Release oils from herbs or juice from fruit

**How**:
1. Place ingredient in bottom of shaker
2. Press and twist gently with muddler
3. For herbs: Gentle (release oils, don't shred)
4. For fruit: Firmer (release juice)

**Examples**: Mojito (mint), Old Fashioned (sugar + bitters)

### 5. Straining

**Single strain**: Use Hawthorne strainer (catches ice)

**Double strain**: Pour through Hawthorne + fine mesh strainer (catches pulp, herbs, ice chips)
- Use for: Cocktails with muddled ingredients, egg whites, or when you want perfectly clear

## Understanding Cocktail Families

### The Six Major Families

Every cocktail fits into one of these categories. Master one from each family, and you understand the fundamentals.

**1. Sours** (Spirit + Citrus + Sweetener)
```
Base spirit + Lemon/Lime + Simple syrup
Ratio: 2 : 1 : 0.75

Examples: Margarita, Daiquiri, Whiskey Sour
```

**2. Highballs** (Spirit + Mixer)
```
Base spirit + Carbonated mixer
Build over ice

Examples: Gin & Tonic, Rum & Coke, Vodka Soda
```

**3. Stirred Cocktails** (Spirit-forward)
```
Base spirit(s) + Modifier (vermouth/liqueur) + Bitters
Ratio varies

Examples: Martini, Manhattan, Negroni
```

**4. Tropical/Tiki** (Complex, often rum-based)
```
Multiple spirits + Juices + Syrups
Often elaborate

Examples: Mai Tai, Piña Colada, Zombie
```

**5. Champagne Cocktails** (Sparkling wine-based)
```
Champagne + Modifier
Build gently

Examples: Mimosa, Aperol Spritz, French 75
```

**6. Old Fashioned Family** (Spirit + Sugar + Bitters)
```
Base spirit + Sugar + Bitters + Water (from ice)
Stirred or built

Examples: Old Fashioned, Sazerac
```

## The Magic Ratios

### The Golden Sour Ratio

**2 : 1 : 0.75** (Spirit : Citrus : Sweetener)

```
2 oz spirit
1 oz fresh citrus juice (lemon or lime)
0.75 oz simple syrup

= Perfect sour every time
```

**This makes**:
- Margarita (tequila + lime)
- Daiquiri (rum + lime)
- Whiskey Sour (whiskey + lemon)
- Gimlet (gin + lime, use lime cordial instead of juice + syrup)

**Adjust to taste**: More sweet (add 0.25 oz syrup), more tart (add 0.25 oz juice)

### The Martini Ratio

**2.5 : 0.5** (Gin : Vermouth) for a dry martini
**2 : 1** for a wet martini

```
2.5 oz gin
0.5 oz dry vermouth

Stir, strain, garnish with olives or lemon twist
```

### The Manhattan Ratio

**2 : 1** (Whiskey : Sweet Vermouth)

```
2 oz bourbon or rye
1 oz sweet vermouth
2 dashes Angostura bitters

Stir, strain, cherry garnish
```

### The Negroni Ratio

**1 : 1 : 1** (Equal parts)

```
1 oz gin
1 oz Campari
1 oz sweet vermouth

Stir, serve over ice, orange peel
```

**Remember these 4 ratios and you can make dozens of cocktails.**

## Essential Ingredients

### Spirits (Start with 3-4)

**Priority 1** (most versatile):
- **Vodka** - Neutral, works in everything
- **Gin** - Botanical, versatile
- **Light rum** - Tropical drinks, daiquiris
- **Tequila (blanco)** - Margaritas, palomas

**Priority 2** (expand your range):
- **Bourbon or rye whiskey** - Old Fashioneds, Manhattans
- **Scotch** - If you like it neat or in a Rob Roy

**Start with 2-3 bottles. Add more as you explore.**

### Modifiers

**Vermouth** (refrigerate after opening):
- Dry vermouth (for martinis)
- Sweet vermouth (for Manhattans, Negronis)

**Liqueurs** (start with 1-2):
- Orange liqueur (Cointreau or Triple Sec) - for Margaritas, Cosmopolitans
- Coffee liqueur (Kahlúa) - for Espresso Martinis, White Russians

**Bitters**:
- Angostura bitters (the essential one)
- Orange bitters (nice to have)

### Fresh Ingredients

**Always fresh** (never bottled):
- Lemons
- Limes

**Herbs**:
- Mint (for Mojitos, Juleps)
- Basil (for smashes)

**Garnishes**:
- Olives (for Martinis)
- Maraschino cherries (for Manhattans)
- Oranges (for Old Fashioneds)

### Syrups and Mixers

**Make at home**:
- **Simple syrup** (1:1 sugar:water, dissolved, cooled)
  - Keeps 1 month refrigerated

**Buy**:
- Tonic water (for G&Ts)
- Club soda (for highballs)
- Ginger beer (for Moscow Mules)

## 10 Foundation Cocktails to Master

Learn these 10 and you understand all the techniques and families.

### 1. Margarita (Sour Family)

**Recipe**:
- 2 oz tequila blanco
- 1 oz fresh lime juice
- 0.75 oz orange liqueur (Cointreau)

**Method**: Shake, strain, serve up or on rocks
**Rim**: Salt (optional)
**Garnish**: Lime wheel

**Why learn it**: Perfect introduction to the sour ratio

---

### 2. Daiquiri (Sour Family)

**Recipe**:
- 2 oz white rum
- 1 oz fresh lime juice
- 0.75 oz simple syrup

**Method**: Shake hard, strain into coupe
**Garnish**: Lime wheel

**Why learn it**: The pure sour - perfect balance showcase

---

### 3. Whiskey Sour (Sour Family)

**Recipe**:
- 2 oz bourbon
- 1 oz fresh lemon juice
- 0.75 oz simple syrup
- (Optional: egg white for foam)

**Method**: Shake (dry shake first if using egg white), strain
**Garnish**: Cherry and orange slice

**Why learn it**: Shows how the sour works with different spirits

---

### 4. Gin & Tonic (Highball Family)

**Recipe**:
- 2 oz gin
- 4-5 oz tonic water

**Method**: Build in highball glass over ice
**Garnish**: Lime wedge

**Why learn it**: Simplest cocktail, all about quality ingredients

---

### 5. Martini (Stirred Family)

**Recipe**:
- 2.5 oz gin (or vodka)
- 0.5 oz dry vermouth

**Method**: Stir with ice for 30 seconds, strain into chilled coupe
**Garnish**: Olives or lemon twist

**Why learn it**: Master of stirring technique, spirit-forward elegance

---

### 6. Manhattan (Stirred Family)

**Recipe**:
- 2 oz rye or bourbon
- 1 oz sweet vermouth
- 2 dashes Angostura bitters

**Method**: Stir with ice, strain into coupe
**Garnish**: Maraschino cherry

**Why learn it**: Classic stirred cocktail, vermouth + bitters balance

---

### 7. Negroni (Stirred Family)

**Recipe**:
- 1 oz gin
- 1 oz Campari
- 1 oz sweet vermouth

**Method**: Stir with ice, serve over ice in rocks glass
**Garnish**: Orange peel

**Why learn it**: Equal parts simplicity, bitter-sweet balance

---

### 8. Old Fashioned (Old Fashioned Family)

**Recipe**:
- 2 oz bourbon or rye
- 1 sugar cube (or 0.5 oz simple syrup)
- 3 dashes Angostura bitters
- Orange peel

**Method**: Muddle sugar + bitters (if using cube), add whiskey, stir with ice
**Garnish**: Orange peel, cherry (optional)

**Why learn it**: Spirit showcase, muddling practice

---

### 9. Mojito (Muddled Family)

**Recipe**:
- 2 oz white rum
- 1 oz fresh lime juice
- 0.75 oz simple syrup
- 8-10 mint leaves
- Club soda (top)

**Method**: Muddle mint gently with syrup, add rum + lime, shake, strain into glass with ice, top with soda
**Garnish**: Mint sprig

**Why learn it**: Herb muddling technique, refreshing summer drink

---

### 10. Espresso Martini (Modern Classic)

**Recipe**:
- 2 oz vodka
- 1 oz coffee liqueur (Kahlúa)
- 1 oz fresh espresso (cooled)

**Method**: Shake hard (creates foam), strain into coupe
**Garnish**: 3 coffee beans

**Why learn it**: Shows how coffee/thick ingredients create texture

## Quick Reference Guide

### When to Shake vs. Stir

**SHAKE** if it contains:
- Juice (citrus, pineapple, etc.)
- Cream or dairy
- Egg white
- Thick syrups

**STIR** if it's:
- All spirits (no juice)
- Spirit + vermouth + bitters
- Want it crystal clear and silky

### Measuring Guide

- **1 oz** = 2 tablespoons = 30 ml
- **0.5 oz** = 1 tablespoon = 15 ml
- **0.75 oz** = 1.5 tablespoons = 22.5 ml
- **Dash** = ⅛ teaspoon (about 6-8 drops)

### Ice Matters

**Use lots of ice**:
- More ice = slower melting = less dilution
- Fill shaker ¾ full with ice
- Fill glass completely with ice

**Ice quality**:
- Large cubes melt slower (best for Old Fashioneds, stirred drinks)
- Regular cubes work fine for shaking
- Avoid crushed ice except for specific drinks (Mint Julep, Swizzles)

### Glassware Temperature

**Chill your glass**:
- Put in freezer for 10 minutes before serving
- Or fill with ice water while making drink

Cold glass = drink stays cold longer

## Tips for Success

### 1. Fresh Juice Always

**Never use bottled citrus juice**. Fresh squeezed makes a 10x difference.

- Lemons and limes last 1-2 weeks
- Juice them as needed (or max 4 hours ahead)

### 2. Simple Syrup is Simple

**Recipe**:
1. Equal parts sugar and water (1 cup each)
2. Heat until sugar dissolves
3. Cool completely
4. Store in fridge (lasts 1 month)

**Variations**:
- Rich simple syrup: 2:1 sugar:water (thicker, sweeter)
- Flavored: Add herbs while heating (rosemary, thyme)

### 3. Taste as You Go

**After making a drink**:
- Taste it before serving
- Too sweet? Add more citrus next time
- Too sour? Add more syrup next time
- Too strong? More ice/dilution next time

Keep notes on what you adjust.

### 4. Mise en Place

**Before making cocktails**:
- Juice your citrus
- Chill your glasses
- Prepare garnishes
- Have ice ready

Makes everything smoother when guests arrive.

### 5. The Garnish Matters

**Orange peel**: Express oils over drink (squeeze over flame for drama)
**Lime/lemon wheel**: Cut notch so it sits on rim
**Herbs**: Slap between hands to release aroma before garnishing

Garnishes aren't just decoration—they add aroma and flavor.

### 6. Build Your Bar Gradually

**Month 1**: Buy vodka + gin, vermouth, bitters, citrus
**Month 2**: Add rum, orange liqueur
**Month 3**: Add whiskey
**Month 4**: Add tequila

Don't buy everything at once. Build as you explore.

### 7. Master One Cocktail at a Time

**Process**:
1. Make the same drink 3-4 times
2. Adjust to your taste
3. Get comfortable with technique
4. Move to next cocktail

Better to make 5 cocktails well than 20 cocktails poorly.

## Cocktail Variations (Once You Master the Basics)

### Sour Variations

**Master**: Margarita (tequila + lime + orange liqueur)

**Variations**:
- **Paloma**: Tequila + grapefruit + lime + soda
- **Sidecar**: Cognac + lemon + orange liqueur
- **Cosmopolitan**: Vodka + lime + cranberry + orange liqueur

**Pattern**: Swap the spirit, keep the structure

### Manhattan Variations

**Master**: Manhattan (whiskey + sweet vermouth + bitters)

**Variations**:
- **Rob Roy**: Scotch instead of whiskey
- **Black Manhattan**: Add Averna (amaro) instead of all vermouth
- **Perfect Manhattan**: Half sweet, half dry vermouth

**Pattern**: Swap the spirit or vermouth type

### Negroni Variations

**Master**: Negroni (gin + Campari + sweet vermouth)

**Variations**:
- **Boulevardier**: Bourbon instead of gin
- **Sbagliato**: Prosecco instead of gin
- **White Negroni**: Gin + Suze + Lillet Blanc

**Pattern**: Equal parts, swap one ingredient

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Not Measuring

**Don't eyeball it**—use a jigger. Consistency matters.

### 2. Using Too Little Ice

More ice = better drink. Fill that shaker.

### 3. Over-Shaking

10-15 seconds is enough. More = too diluted and watery.

### 4. Not Tasting

Taste your drink before serving. Adjust if needed.

### 5. Buying Too Much

Start small. 3-4 spirits covers most classics.

### 6. Ignoring Technique

Shake when you should stir = cloudy Martini
Stir when you should shake = flat Margarita

Technique matters.
