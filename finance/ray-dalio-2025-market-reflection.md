## Introduction: Looking Beyond the Headlines

**The Conventional Story**: US stocks, especially AI stocks, were the best investments of 2025. The S&P 500 returned 18%. Tech dominates. America winning.

**Ray Dalio's Story**: The biggest story of 2025 was actually the decline in the value of money (particularly the dollar), and US stocks massively underperformed both international stocks and gold when you look at them correctly.

Both stories use the same facts and numbers. So how can they be so different?

The answer reveals something fundamental about how money, markets, and wealth really work—and why the lens through which you view returns matters enormously.

This tutorial breaks down Dalio's analysis of 2025 markets, explaining the core concepts in plain language. You'll understand:
- Why currency matters more than you think
- How to really measure investment returns
- The mechanics driving markets, politics, and economies
- The "Big Cycle" framework Dalio uses to understand where we are

**Who is Ray Dalio?** Founder of Bridgewater Associates (world's largest hedge fund), systematic macro investor, author of *Principles* and *How Countries Go Broke: The Big Cycle*. He studies economic patterns across history to understand cause-effect relationships.

Let's start with the most important concept: the value of money itself.

## The Real Story: The Value of Money

### What Really Happened in 2025

**The headline**: S&P 500 returned 18% in 2025.

**The reality**: Against what? Returns are always measured relative to something—usually your home currency. But what if your currency itself is losing value?

**Dollar performance in 2025**:
- Down 0.3% vs Japanese yen
- Down 4% vs Chinese renminbi
- Down 12% vs euro
- Down 13% vs Swiss franc
- **Down 39% vs gold**

In other words: **all fiat currencies fell in 2025, and the dollar fell significantly against stronger currencies and gold**.

### The Key Insight

When your currency declines, it creates an illusion. Things *measured* in that currency appear to rise, but you're not necessarily getting richer—your measuring stick is getting smaller.

**Analogy**: Imagine you're measuring your height in inches, but inches are secretly shrinking. You measure yourself as 72 inches tall this year, 75 inches next year. Are you growing? Or are inches shrinking? If you measured in centimeters (which stayed constant), you'd see you're the same height.

Same with investments. If the dollar shrinks 10%, and your stocks go up 10% in dollar terms, you haven't actually gained wealth—you just maintained it.

### The Same S&P 500, Measured in Different Currencies

Here's what the S&P 500's 18% dollar return looks like from different perspectives:

| Currency Perspective | S&P 500 Return |
|---------------------|----------------|
| US Dollar | +18% |
| Japanese Yen | +17% |
| Chinese Renminbi | +13% |
| Euro | +4% |
| Swiss Franc | +3% |
| **Gold** | **-28%** |

**Same investment. Same year. Completely different results depending on your lens.**

For a European investor, US stocks barely moved. For someone measuring in gold (the hardest currency), US stocks *crashed* by 28%.

This is why Dalio says: **"The biggest story of 2025 was what happened to the value of money."**

## Currency Basics: Fiat vs Hard Money

To understand what happened, you need to understand two types of money:

### Fiat Currency

**Fiat currency** = money created by government decree ("fiat" = Latin for "let it be done").

Examples: US dollar, euro, yen, pound, renminbi

**Key characteristics**:
- Can be printed/created at will by central banks
- Not backed by physical commodities
- Value comes from government backing and trust
- Supply is controlled by monetary policy

**The Trade-off**:
- Pro: Flexible. Governments can stimulate economy by creating more money
- Con: Can be debased. Creating too much money reduces its value

### Hard Currency (Gold)

**Hard currency** = money that's difficult to increase in supply, holds value over time.

**Gold** is the primary hard currency because:
- Can't be printed or created easily
- Supply grows slowly (~2% per year from mining)
- Has been a store of value for thousands of years
- Second largest reserve currency globally (after the dollar)

**The Trade-off**:
- Pro: Maintains value. Can't be debased by government policy
- Con: Inflexible. Can't stimulate economy by "printing" more gold

### The Pattern

Throughout history, when governments run large deficits and central banks print money to finance them:
1. Fiat currencies lose value
2. Hard currencies (gold, silver) gain value
3. The purchasing power of savings in fiat currency erodes

**2025 was a textbook example of this pattern playing out.**

## How Currency Movements Change Everything

When your currency declines, three major things happen:

### 1. Your Wealth and Buying Power Decrease

If you hold dollar-denominated assets and the dollar falls 12% against the euro:
- Your wealth decreased 12% relative to Europeans
- European goods cost you 12% more
- Your international purchasing power fell

**Real impact**: That European vacation is now more expensive. Imported goods cost more. Your global wealth is diminished.

### 2. International Trade Shifts

When the dollar weakens:
- **US exports become cheaper** for foreigners (stimulates US manufacturing)
- **Imports become more expensive** for Americans (domestic inflation)
- **Trade balances shift** over time (with a lag)

This is why Trump's policies to weaken the dollar support US manufacturing—it makes American goods more competitive globally.

### 3. Investment Returns Are Relative to Your Currency

**Critical question**: What currency do you ultimately need to spend in?

If you're American and will spend in dollars, measuring returns in dollars makes sense. But if you're:
- European needing euros
- Chinese needing renminbi
- Global and diversified

Then dollar returns are misleading. You need to account for currency movements.

### The Currency Hedging Question

**Dalio's advice**: "You should always be hedged to your least-risk currency mix and make tactical moves from there if you think you are capable of making them well."

**What does this mean?**

If you don't have a strong view on currencies, don't accidentally make a big bet by being 100% in one currency. Instead:
- Hold a diversified mix of currencies
- Match your currency exposure to where you'll spend money
- Only deviate if you have real conviction

**Example**: If you live in the US but have assets only in dollars, you're making a concentrated bet on the dollar. If it falls, your entire wealth falls. A more balanced approach: some dollar assets, some euro, some gold, etc.

## Asset Performance in 2025: The Full Picture

Now let's look at how different assets performed, understanding currency effects.

### Best Investment: Gold

**Gold returned 65% in dollar terms** (or +39% adjusted for dollar decline).

Why?
1. **Monetary debasement**: Central banks printed money → fiat currencies weakened → gold strengthened
2. **Geopolitical uncertainty**: Sanctions, conflicts → demand for neutral reserve asset
3. **Diversification**: Foreign investors reduced dollar exposure → bought gold
4. **Supply/demand**: Gold supply grows ~2%/year, demand surged

**Gold outperformed the S&P 500 by 47 percentage points**—the biggest divergence in years.

### US Stocks: Strong in Dollars, Weak Relatively

**S&P 500 total return: 18%** (in dollars)

Breakdown:
- Earnings growth: 12%
- P/E multiple expansion: ~5%
- Dividend yield: ~1%
- **Total: ~18%**

**Sounds great, right?** But context matters:

1. **Currency-adjusted**: Only +3% in Swiss francs, -28% in gold
2. **Relative to other markets**: Underperformed Europe, China, UK, Japan significantly

The S&P 500 *did* well in absolute terms (earnings grew, economy expanded), but it was the *worst performing major market* when you account for currency.

### US Stocks vs International: The Big Reversal

For years, US stocks outperformed international stocks. 2025 reversed this trend dramatically:

| Market | Outperformance vs US Stocks |
|--------|----------------------------|
| European stocks | +23% |
| Chinese stocks | +21% |
| UK stocks | +19% |
| Japanese stocks | +10% |
| Emerging markets overall | +16% (34% return vs 18%) |

**Why the reversal?**
1. **Valuation**: US stocks were expensive (high P/E ratios), non-US stocks were cheap
2. **Currency**: Dollar weakened, making non-US returns better
3. **Diversification**: Investors rebalanced away from overweight US exposure
4. **Geopolitical risk**: Trump's sanctions and conflicts scared foreign investors
5. **Relative growth**: Other economies growing, catching up to US

### Bonds: Terrible in Real Terms

**10-year US Treasury bonds returned 9% in dollars** (~half from yield, half from price appreciation)

But measured in harder currencies:
- +9% in yen
- +5% in renminbi
- **-4% in euros**
- **-4% in Swiss francs**
- **-34% in gold**

**Why bonds performed poorly**:
- When currency loses value, bonds (promises to deliver that currency) lose real worth
- Foreign investors didn't want to hold dollar bonds unless currency-hedged
- Rising supply of debt ($10 trillion to be rolled over) pressures prices

**Cash was even worse**—no yield to offset currency decline.

### The Complete Picture

In 2025, the winners were:
1. **Gold** (hard currency, globally neutral)
2. **Non-US stocks** (better valuations, currency gains)
3. **Emerging market assets** (diversification away from US)

The losers were:
1. **US dollar cash** (pure currency exposure)
2. **US bonds** (currency risk + coming supply/demand imbalance)
3. **Illiquid assets** (venture capital, private equity, real estate)

## Understanding Market Mechanics

To understand why markets moved the way they did, you need to understand a few key concepts.

### Concept 1: P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings)

**P/E Ratio** = Stock Price ÷ Earnings per Share

This tells you how much investors pay for each dollar of earnings.

**Example**:
- Company earns $5 per share
- Stock trades at $100
- P/E = 100 ÷ 5 = 20

Interpretation: You're paying $20 for every $1 of annual earnings.

**High P/E** (like 30+): Expensive. Investors expect high future growth.
**Low P/E** (like 10): Cheap. Either low expectations or value opportunity.

**S&P 500 in 2025**: P/E expanded by ~5%, meaning stocks got more expensive relative to earnings. This contributed to the 18% return beyond the 12% earnings growth.

### Concept 2: Equity Risk Premium

**Equity Risk Premium** = Expected return from stocks - Risk-free rate (bonds)

This is the extra return investors demand for taking the risk of owning stocks vs safe government bonds.

**When the premium is high**: Stocks are cheap relative to bonds (attractive)
**When the premium is low**: Stocks are expensive relative to bonds (risky)

**Dalio's calculation for current market**:
- Long-term expected stock return: ~4.7% (very low, sub-10th percentile historically)
- 10-year bond yield: ~4.9%
- **Equity risk premium: NEGATIVE** (stocks expected to return less than bonds)

**What this means**: Stocks are expensive relative to the risk. If you can get 4.9% risk-free from bonds, why take stock market risk for only 4.7% expected return?

### Concept 3: Credit Spreads

**Credit spread** = Yield on corporate bonds - Yield on government bonds

This measures the extra interest companies must pay over the "risk-free" government rate.

**Narrow spreads** (like in 2025): Investors aren't demanding much extra yield for risk → they're comfortable taking risk → risk assets are expensive
**Wide spreads**: Investors are scared → demanding high yields → risk assets are cheap

**In 2025, credit spreads narrowed to very low levels**, which:
- Pushed stock and bond prices up (good for 2025 returns)
- Left little room for further gains (bad for future returns)
- Increased risk of reversal if conditions worsen

### Concept 4: Fiscal and Monetary Policy

**Fiscal policy** = Government spending and taxation
**Monetary policy** = Central bank actions (interest rates, money printing)

**What happened in 2025**:
1. **Fiscal**: Trump administration ran large deficits, cut regulations, provided subsidies to key industries → stimulated economy
2. **Monetary**: Fed cut interest rates, eased credit availability → lowered discount rates, boosted asset prices

**The mechanism**:
- Lower interest rates → Future cash flows worth more today → Stock prices rise
- Easier credit → Companies borrow to invest/buyback stock → Earnings rise
- More money in system → Dollar's value falls → Asset prices rise in dollar terms

This is **reflation**: deliberately inflating asset prices and economy through policy.

### The Valuation Problem

Dalio's concern: **All the easy gains have been captured.**

- P/E ratios high → No more multiple expansion available
- Credit spreads narrow → No more spread compression available
- Interest rates low → But debt supply increasing, may push rates up

**If interest rates rise** (due to supply/demand imbalance as $10 trillion in debt needs refinancing):
- Stock valuations compress (future cash flows worth less)
- Credit spreads widen (riskier loans less attractive)
- Both stocks and bonds could fall together

**This is why Dalio is cautious on US assets going forward.**

## The Political-Economic Connection

Markets don't operate in a vacuum—politics drives policy, which drives markets, which affects voters, which drives politics. It's a feedback loop.

### The 2025 Political-Economic Story

**Trump's first year with control of both houses**:
- Aggressive bet on capitalism and deregulation
- Massive fiscal stimulus (government spending)
- Tariffs to protect domestic production and raise revenue
- Government-directed capitalism (not free-market capitalism)
- Support for AI, manufacturing, key industries

**The results**:
- Strong economic growth (7% sales growth for S&P 500 companies)
- Margin expansion (profits grew faster than sales)
- Stock market gains concentrated among capitalists (top 10% who own most stocks)

### The Wealth Gap Problem

**Who benefited**:
- Top 10% ("haves") who own stocks → wealth increased significantly
- Capitalists/business owners → margins improved, stocks up

**Who didn't**:
- Bottom 60% → limited stock ownership, hurt by inflation
- Workers → didn't capture much of the productivity gains

**The math**: Of the 12% earnings growth:
- 57% came from sales increasing (growing pie)
- 43% came from margin expansion (more going to owners, less to workers)

**The political consequence**:

**Top 10%**: "Economy is great! No inflation problem! Capitalism works!"

**Bottom 60%**: "I can't afford anything! Inflation is crushing me! System is rigged!"

### The Coming Political Fight

**Dalio's prediction**: Value of money (affordability) will be the #1 political issue.

**The setup**:
- **Hard right** (Trump, pro-capitalism): Keep margins high, support business owners, cut regulations
- **Hard left** (Bernie Sanders, AOC, democratic socialists): Tax billionaires, raise wages, redistribute wealth

**The fight**: Who captures future productivity gains?
- Option A: Companies keep improving margins → profits to shareholders → stock prices up → capitalists win
- Option B: Workers demand higher wages, government raises taxes → margins compress → workers/socialists win

**Markets are currently pricing Option A**—but political forces are organizing around Option B.

### The Democratic Cycle Problem

**The pattern**:
1. Party wins election with big promises
2. Implements extreme policies
3. Can't meet voter expectations (too ambitious, too little time)
4. Gets thrown out in next election
5. Other party takes over, promises opposite extreme
6. Repeat

**The problem**: No party stays in power long enough to implement lasting change. Trump has a 2-year mandate (until 2026 midterms could flip the House), then faces headwinds.

This instability is becoming the norm in developed democracies—similar to what used to happen in underdeveloped countries. It's destabilizing for long-term planning and investment.

### Investment Implication

**The political risk**: Markets are priced for continued margin expansion and profit growth. But if the left gains power and redistributes wealth (higher wages, higher taxes), margins compress and stock valuations fall.

**The uncertainty**: Will companies be allowed to keep improving margins, or will political forces capture those gains?

This is a major variable for future returns.

## Geopolitics and the Changing World Order

2025 saw a fundamental shift in how the world operates.

### From Multilateralism to Unilateralism

**Multilateralism** (post-WWII order):
- Countries aspire to follow international rules
- Multilateral organizations oversee (UN, WTO, IMF)
- Cooperation and negotiation valued
- Predictable, stable, rule-based

**Unilateralism** (emerging order):
- Power rules, might makes right
- Countries act in pure self-interest
- Threats, sanctions, protectionism normal
- Unpredictable, unstable, strength-based

**2025 marked the clear shift to unilateralism**, driven by Trump's "America First" approach.

### Consequences of the Shift

**1. Military spending increases**:
- Countries feel less safe
- Can't rely on international rules or alliances
- Must build own military capabilities
- More government borrowing to finance defense

**2. Economic weaponization**:
- Sanctions used freely as foreign policy tool
- Threatens of tariffs and trade restrictions
- Economic warfare becomes normal
- Countries diversify away from dollar to avoid sanctions risk

**3. Deglobalization**:
- Supply chains move onshore
- Less international trade
- More protectionism (tariffs, quotas)
- Regional blocs replace global integration

**4. Capital flows shift**:
- Foreign investors scared of US sanctions
- Reduced demand for US assets (bonds, stocks, dollars)
- Increased demand for neutral assets (gold)
- Rebalancing toward diversification

### The Gold Connection

**Why gold benefited**:
- Can't be sanctioned (no government controls it)
- Globally neutral (not tied to any country)
- Serves as reserve asset when dollar is weaponized
- Central banks and investors diversifying away from dollar risk

When the world order is unstable and the reserve currency (dollar) is used as a weapon, gold becomes the safe haven.

### The Broader Implications

**Rising risks**:
- More potential for conflicts (economic and military)
- Less predictable international relations
- Higher costs (military spending, deglobalization)
- More volatility in markets

**Investment implications**:
- Diversification across countries/currencies more important
- Gold and hard assets gain appeal
- US assets face headwinds from foreign selling
- Emerging markets and regional powers gain relevance

## The Big Cycle Framework

Dalio's work is built on studying historical patterns—what he calls "The Big Cycle."

### What is the Big Cycle?

**The Big Cycle** describes how countries rise and fall over long periods (~50-100 years), driven by five major forces:

1. **Debt/Money/Markets/Economy**
2. **Internal Politics** (wealth gaps, political conflicts)
3. **Geopolitics** (international power dynamics)
4. **Acts of Nature** (climate, pandemics, disasters)
5. **Technology** (innovation driving productivity)

These forces interact and compound over time, creating predictable patterns.

### The Debt/Money Cycle (Force #1)

**The pattern**:

**Phase 1: Rise** (Building Prosperity)
- Country produces more than it consumes
- Savings accumulate, debt is low
- Currency is strong, trusted globally
- Lends to other countries
- Example: US after WWII, China 2000-2015

**Phase 2: Peak** (Living on Credit)
- Becomes wealthy, lifestyle expands
- Starts consuming more than producing
- Borrows to maintain standard of living
- Debt grows, but still manageable
- Example: US 1980-2008

**Phase 3: Decline** (Debt Burden Grows)
- Debt becomes unsustainable
- Must print money to service debt
- Currency weakens
- Foreign lenders lose trust
- Example: US 2008-present, especially accelerating in 2020s

**Phase 4: Crisis** (Debt Restructuring)
- Debt crisis or currency crisis
- Default, high inflation, or both
- Significant wealth loss
- System resets
- Example: Weimar Germany 1920s, Argentina repeatedly

**Where is the US?** Dalio suggests: Late Phase 3, potentially approaching Phase 4.

Evidence:
- Large and growing deficits ($2+ trillion/year)
- $36+ trillion in total debt (130% of GDP)
- Printing money (Fed balance sheet expansion)
- Dollar weakening against hard currencies
- Foreign demand for US debt declining

### The Political Cycle (Force #2)

**The pattern**:

**Phase 1: Cooperation**
- Shared prosperity, middle class strong
- Political center holds, compromise works
- Wealth gaps moderate
- Social cohesion high

**Phase 2: Polarization**
- Wealth gaps widen
- Different groups have different economic realities
- Political extremes gain power
- Compromise breaks down

**Phase 3: Conflict**
- Extreme wealth inequality
- Populist movements (left and right)
- Political fights over wealth redistribution
- Potential for internal conflict

**Where is the US?** Entering Phase 3.

Evidence:
- Extreme wealth concentration (top 10% own ~70% of stocks)
- Populist movements on both sides (Trump right, Sanders/AOC left)
- Inability to compromise politically
- 2025: Setting up for "big fight" between hard right and hard left

### The Geopolitical Cycle (Force #3)

**The pattern**:

**Phase 1: Dominant Power**
- One country clearly strongest (military, economic)
- Sets rules, maintains order
- Other countries accept dominance
- Example: US 1945-2000

**Phase 2: Rising Challenger**
- Emerging power grows stronger
- Challenges existing order
- Tensions increase
- Example: China 2000-present

**Phase 3: Conflict**
- Direct competition for dominance
- Economic or military conflict
- World order reshapes
- Example: US-China rivalry now escalating

**Where are we?** Late Phase 2, potentially entering Phase 3.

Evidence:
- China's rise economically and militarily
- Shift from multilateralism to unilateralism
- Economic sanctions and trade wars
- Increased military spending globally
- Deglobalization and regional blocs forming

### Technology (Force #4)

Technology is the wild card—it can accelerate or disrupt all other cycles.

**Current state**: AI boom creating massive productivity potential.

**The questions**:
1. How much will AI actually improve productivity?
2. Who captures the gains—capitalists or workers?
3. Does it create winner-take-all dynamics?
4. Does it help solve debt problem (growth) or make it worse (displacement)?

**Dalio's view**: AI is in early stages of a bubble, but underlying technology is real and transformative.

### Acts of Nature (Force #5)

**Current state**: Climate change progressing, increasing costs and disruptions.

**2025 development**: Trump administration shifted away from climate spending, focused on energy production.

**Long-term**: Climate will continue to be a force requiring resources and adaptation.

### How the Cycles Interact

The five forces compound:
- Large debt → print money → currency weakens → wealth gaps widen → political conflict → geopolitical tensions → more military spending → more debt → repeat

**The Big Cycle template suggests**: When a dominant power reaches late stages of debt cycle, with internal political conflict and external geopolitical challenges, it typically faces a crisis and restructuring that leads to a new order.

**Dalio's message**: The US and much of the developed world are following this pattern. Understanding it helps you prepare.

**For investors who want to be active**:

**The Big Picture**:
1. **We're late in the debt cycle**: Unsustainable debt, printing money, currency debasement
2. **Political conflict is rising**: Wealth gaps widening, extremes gaining power, fight over distribution ahead
3. **Geopolitical order is shifting**: From cooperation to conflict, multilateralism to unilateralism
4. **Technology is accelerating**: AI boom creating productivity but also bubbles and displacement
5. **Nature is pressuring**: Climate costs mounting

**These five forces are compounding**, following historical patterns Dalio has studied across empires and countries for centuries.

**The Pattern Suggests**: Periods like this—high debt, political polarization, geopolitical tension, technological disruption—typically lead to some form of crisis and restructuring. Not necessarily catastrophic, but significant. A resetting of the system.

**For Investors**: This isn't a time for complacency or extrapolating the past. It's a time for:
- Diversification (countries, currencies, assets)
- Hard assets as insurance (gold, real assets)
- Lower return expectations
- Understanding political and geopolitical risks
- Systematic thinking over emotional reactions

**The Opportunity**: If you understand the cycles and mechanics, you can position for what's coming rather than being surprised by it. Dalio's framework gives you that lens.

**The Challenge**: Most people won't do this. They'll keep 100% in dollar-denominated US assets, assume the past predicts the future, and be caught off-guard when the cycles turn.

**The Choice**: Understand the Big Cycle and prepare, or ignore it and hope it doesn't matter.
